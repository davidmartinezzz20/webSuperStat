How to keep a handball match sheet offline

16 September 2026

Anyone who has ever kept the stats for a handball match knows the problem: the sports hall has no wifi, your phone loses signal the moment you walk through the door, and the match will not wait for you to get it back. So the old standby has been paper — quick to get out, but slow to fill in properly and, once the match gets tense, easy to abandon halfway through.

This guide goes over what is worth logging during a match, why paper falls short as soon as you want more out of it than the final score, and how to do it from your phone even without a single bar of signal.

What to log during the match (beyond the score)

The final score says very little. What actually helps you prepare the next training session or the next knockout tie is finer grained:

  • Shots on goal, with their outcome —goal, save or off target— and the exact spot they were taken from.
  • Goalkeeper saves, by area of the goal, so you know where they are strongest and where there is work to do.
  • Turnovers, to see how many each player gives away match after match.
  • Two-minute suspensions, to keep track of the team’s discipline.
  • Court position for every shot, which over time becomes a heat map per player and per season.

None of this replaces the official match sheet that the team officer or the referee fills in for the federation — that is the one that certifies the result and the sanctions to the competition. This is the coaching staff’s own record: the data the team needs in order to improve, not the data the rules demand.

Why paper falls short

On paper you can jot down almost all of it, but three problems show up as soon as you want to really use it:

  1. Time. Writing down shot position, outcome and player by hand, with the match under way, forces you to choose between watching the game and watching the paper.
  2. Transcribing. What you wrote in the stands is worth nothing until someone types it into a spreadsheet — usually days later, when part of the context (and the enthusiasm) is already gone.
  3. Comparing seasons. Adding up the stats of 20 matches by hand to see how a player is developing is doable once. Doing it every week, not so much.

How to do it from your phone, with no signal

The trick is not to replace paper with a spreadsheet on your phone —that has exactly the same time problem—. The trick is an app built to be used with the match under way, phone in hand, that does not depend on having a signal:

  1. You open the match before it starts. Offline, with no waiting.
  2. You log with taps, not with a keyboard. One tap for the kind of action (shot, save, turnover, suspension), another for the area on the court or goal diagram. No typing during the match.
  3. You can undo. If you pick the wrong area or the wrong player, an undo button keeps a slip from being recorded for good.
  4. Everything is saved on the phone. No wifi, no mobile data, no depending on the sports hall having coverage.
  5. By the final whistle the report is done. Result, how the score evolved, per-player stats and a shot map, ready to go over in the changing room or on the way home.
  6. It syncs by itself as soon as there is a signal. Nothing to remember to upload, nothing to move anywhere.

What to do with the data after the match

Once you have several matches logged, the individual report starts to matter less than the running totals:

  • Season stats per player —shots, efficiency, saves— without adding anything up by hand.
  • A heat map of where each player shoots from and where they score, useful both for individual training and for scouting the opposition.
  • How the score evolved match by match, to spot patterns (does the team fade in the second half? does it always come back at the end?).
  • CSV export, in case the club wants to cross this data with another tool.

Who this is for

Mostly for coaches and statisticians at youth and amateur clubs, where there is no dedicated analyst and no budget for professional scouting software, but there is an appetite for knowing more than the final score. Also for clubs and regional federations that want to digitise how they follow their teams without asking anyone to learn a complicated tool.
